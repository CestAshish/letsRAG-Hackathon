--- QUESTION 4 ---
Q: Does it apply to my biometric data?
EXPECTED: This is ambiguous because 'it' is not defined (could refer to a specific system or the regulation as a whole) and 'my' does not provide the context of where or how the biometric data is being processed, which is crucial for determining how the regulation applies.
ACTUAL: Yes. The EU AI Act specifically addresses the processing of biometric data.  
- **Article 3** defines “biometric data” as personal data derived from physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics (e.g., facial images, fingerprints).  
- The Act classifies AI systems that use biometric data for identification, verification, categorisation or emotion‑recognition as **high‑risk** (Recital 54) and, in certain cases, **prohibited** (Recital 30).  
- For law‑enforcement use of real‑time remote biometric identification, the Act imposes strict safeguards and authorisation requirements (Recital 38).  

Therefore, if your biometric data is processed by an AI system—especially for identification, verification, categorisation, or emotion‑recognition—the EU AI Act applies to that processing.  

References: [Article 3](#chunk-1), [Recital 30](#chunk-5), [Recital 38](#chunk-4)

--- QUESTION 5 ---
Q: When does the law start applying to the systems and models?
EXPECTED: Could you please clarify which specific systems or models you are referring to? The AI Act has different application dates (e.g., 2 August 2026 for general provisions, 2 February 2025 for prohibitions, 2 August 2025 for general-purpose AI models and governance, etc.).
ACTUAL: The EU AI Act becomes applicable to AI systems and models on the following dates:

| Provision | Effective date | What it covers |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| **Full regulation** (all obligations, conformity‑assessment, governance, etc.) | **2 August 2026** | Applies to all AI systems and models that are placed on the market or put into service after this date. |
| **Prohibited practices** (e.g., emotion‑recognition in the workplace, social scoring, etc.) | **2 February 2025** | These prohibitions take effect earlier to mitigate unacceptable risks. |
| **Governance and enforcement framework** (notified bodies, conformity‑assessment procedures, etc.) | **2 August 2025** | The infrastructure needed to enforce the regulation must be operational by this date. |
| **Obligations for providers of general‑purpose AI models** | **2 August 2025** | Providers must comply with the regulation’s requirements for general‑purpose models placed on the market before this date. |
| **Administrative‑fine regime** | **2 August 2025** | Penalties for non‑compliance become enforceable from this date. |

These dates are set in the preamble and recitals of the regulation (see Recital 178‑180).

--- QUESTION 13 ---
Q: What is the maximum fine if they don't give the requested information?
EXPECTED: The question is ambiguous as it does not specify who 'they' are or what 'information' is being requested. It could refer to providers of general-purpose AI models failing to provide information under Article 91 or 92 (fines up to EUR 15 000 000 or 3% of turnover), or general operators failing to provide information to notified bodies or national competent authorities (fines up to EUR 7 500 000 or 1% of turnover).
ACTUAL: The EU AI Act sets the maximum fine for a provider of a general‑purpose AI model that fails to comply with a request for documentation or information (Article 101) at **the higher of:**

* **3 % of the provider’s total worldwide turnover in the preceding financial year**, or  
* **EUR 15 000 000**.

Thus, the fine can reach up to the greater of those two amounts.

ACTUAL: Yes.

These provisions mean that the relevant AI systems are subject to evaluation and compliance checks that culminate in 2030 (and beyond).
